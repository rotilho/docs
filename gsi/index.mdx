# Growth Stability Index

The published Growth Stability Index (GSI) design scales configured Atto distribution rates in response to market data. The displayed GSI is **<Metric name="distribution.growth-stability-index" />**. Figures refresh from [Atto's metrics service](/metrics) when available; otherwise the page shows its last cached values. In the published design, a covered route's configured base rate is multiplied by the GSI. Which routes are currently covered, together with the applied configuration and update cadence, must be confirmed from current operational records before relying on them.

:::note Scope of the GSI
The GSI paces the release of pre-created Atto and is intended to seek lower expected volatility than a fixed release schedule. It does not create a peg, price floor or band, reserve, redemption right, guaranteed liquidity, or guaranteed price outcome.
:::

## How It Works

In the published design, the GSI is a value between a configured minimum (described as typically 0.10) and 1.0 that represents the market position relative to recent price history. The design uses three price metrics:

- **7-Day Average Price**: A smoothed price that reduces noise from short-term fluctuations
- **All-Time High (ATH)**: The highest price Atto has ever reached
- **1-Month Low**: The lowest price in the past 30 days

### The Formula

```
GSI = (7-Day Average Price - 1-Month Low) / (ATH - 1-Month Low)
```

This formula positions a market-price input on a scale from 0 to 1:
- **GSI near 0**: Price is close to the 1-month low (bearish conditions)
- **GSI near 1**: Price is close to the all-time high (bullish conditions)

## Asymmetric Adjustment

A key feature of the published GSI design is its **asymmetric adjustment mechanism**:

- **Target decreases**: The design applies a lower target value on the next update
- **Target increases**: The design caps an increase at 0.01 per update

This asymmetry is intended to **reduce covered distribution rates quickly** after lower price readings while **restoring them gradually** after higher readings. The current update cadence and applied parameters require confirmation from current operational records.

### Example

Consider a scenario where:
- Current GSI: 0.50
- Market rallies, target GSI becomes: 0.80

Under the published design, instead of jumping to 0.80, the GSI would increase gradually:
- Update 1: 0.50 → 0.51
- Update 2: 0.51 → 0.52
- ... and so on

If a later price reading instead produces a target of 0.30:
- The design applies the lower target on the next update: 0.52 → 0.30

## Impact on Distribution

Under the published design, the GSI influences the rate at which existing Atto enters circulation through covered distribution programmes. Current programme mappings must be confirmed from operational records. For a route that is covered by the design:

- **High (closer to 1.0)**: Covered distribution rates can be closer to their configured base rates
- **Low (closer to the configured minimum)**: Covered distribution rates are scaled down further

The asymmetric adjustment is designed to limit how quickly covered rates can rise after a reduction.

## What the GSI Provides

As a published design, the GSI provides a consistent, visible input for covered distribution routes:

- **Responsive settings**: Covered rates respond to the price metrics used in the formula
- **Bounded scaling**: The configured minimum and maximum constrain the multiplier
- **Gradual recovery**: The upward adjustment cap slows increases after rates have fallen
